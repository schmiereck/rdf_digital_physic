### 1. Executive Summary
We have achieved a major research milestone in the "Emergence of Digital Physics" project. By simulating a moving mass source on a 3D+1 D4 (FCC) discrete spacetime lattice, we have observed and quantified the emergent equivalents of **gravitational frame dragging (light dragging)** and **Doppler-like Shapiro delay asymmetry**.

### 2. Key Technical Achievements
*   **Dynamic Spacetime LGCA Validation:** Demonstrated a 100% bit-conserving, reversible 3D+1 D4 Spacetime Lattice Gas Cellular Automaton coupled with a dynamic, translating mass well ($v_y = 0.2$).
*   **Emergent Frame Dragging:** Quantified that photons passing close to a moving mass well are dynamically deflected by up to $+16.97$ spatial lattice units in the direction of the mass's translation. *   **Doppler-Like Shapiro Delay:** Validated that photons traveling in the same direction as the mass well undergo a larger Shapiro coordinate delay ($+1.46$ steps) than those traveling counter to it, because they dwell longer within the translating high-potential region.
*   **Implicit Geodesic Solver:** Implemented and validated a time-dependent Dijkstra shortest-path algorithm. The implicit coordinate arrival-time equation was solved using a fast, 3-step fixed-point iteration, converging to a residual of $< 10^{-9}$.
