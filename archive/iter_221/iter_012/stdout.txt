## Why a "net velocity ~1.0" can pass a `max_velocity_threshold` of 0.9

The confusion stems from **two different velocity calculations** in the code:

| Metric | Formula | Meaning |
|--------|---------|---------|
| `mean_velocity_magnitude` (checked against threshold) | `‖mean(vector)‖` = `√(mean_dx² + mean_dy²)` | Magnitude of the **mean velocity vector** across all windows |
| Mean of magnitudes (not checked) | `mean(‖vector‖)` = `mean(window_velocity_mags)` | Average speed regardless of direction |

The critical distinction: **the code checks the magnitude of the mean vector, not the mean of the magnitudes**.

When per-window velocity vectors point in different directions, they partially cancel out in the mean, producing a `mean_velocity_magnitude` much smaller than the individual window speeds.

### Concrete example

Suppose a drifter with 5 windows, each with speed ~1.0 but varying direction:

| Window | Velocity vector | Magnitude |
|--------|----------------|-----------|
| 1 | `( 1.0,  0.0)` | 1.0 |
| 2 | `( 0.0,  1.0)` | 1.0 |
| 3 | `(-1.0,  0.0)` | 1.0 |
| 4 | `( 0.0, -1.0)` | 1.0 |
| 5 | `( 1.0,  0.0)` | 1.0 |

- **Mean of magnitudes** (what you might call "net velocity"): `(1+1+1+1+1)/5 = 1.0` → appears to fail 0.9 threshold
- **Mean vector**: `( (1+0-1+0+1)/5 , (0+1+0-1+0)/5 ) = (0.2, 0.0)`
- **`mean_velocity_magnitude`** (what the code actually checks): `√(0.2² + 0) = 0.2` → **passes** the 0.9 threshold!

### How the code defeats this

The real penalty comes at **step 4 (lines 365–367)** where the standard deviation of velocity magnitudes is computed:

```python
velocity_magnitudes = np.array(window_velocity_mags, dtype=np.float64)
std_dev_velocity_magnitudes = float(np.std(velocity_magnitudes))
```

For the example above, `std_dev ≈ 0.45`, and the final fitness is:

```
fitness = 0.2 / (1 + 0.45) ≈ 0.14
```

So even though this rule bypassed the `max_velocity_threshold` check, its fitness is suppressed by the **consistency penalty** in the denominator — exactly by design, to prevent drifters from scoring well.
