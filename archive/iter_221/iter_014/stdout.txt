## Why the v=1c Glider Was Likely *Not* Rejected

The key reason: **the centre of mass (COM) velocity does NOT equal the pattern's propagation speed.**

A v=1c glider is a pattern where the *shape* advances 1 cell per generation, but its **COM moves much slower** because:

1. The glider is an extended object — its live cells span multiple rows/columns
2. COM = weighted average of all cell positions. As the pattern shifts, the COM only moves fractionally each step
3. For a typical glider, the COM velocity is roughly `1 / N` where N is related to the bounding-box dimensions and the glider's mass distribution

For example, a classic "glider" in Game of Life (5 cells) moving diagonally at v=1c has a COM velocity of approximately **0.5 cells/step** diagonally (sqrt(0.5² + 0.5²) ≈ 0.707), which is **below 0.9** and therefore **not rejected**.

In other words, **the `max_velocity_threshold` check is measuring COM drift speed, not pattern propagation speed**, so it cannot directly catch light-speed gliders — a v=1c pattern will have a COM velocity well under 0.9c and will pass this filter. The rejection of light-speed objects must come from other parts of the fitness pipeline (e.g., separate light-speed gates, or the fact that the consistency metric expects v < c behavior).
