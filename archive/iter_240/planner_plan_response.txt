## Scientific Rationale and Strategic Pivot

Following the outcomes of Phase 5 (where the emergent dynamic coordinate-latency gravity was subjected to a rigorous open-boundary audit in iter_238 and refuted due to sub-pixel discretization noise and broken coordinate covariance), and the completion of the classical 2D hex glider scattering characterization in iter_239, we are pivoting to **Phase 7: Particle Zoo (Phase 7.1 — Glider Taxonomy)**.

To build a complete digital physics, we must catalog and classify the spectrum of stable propagating patterns (gliders) on highly symmetric grids. In iter_224, we established a 3D FCC cellular automata engine with a 12-channel cuboctahedron neighborhood and identified `LUT-08` as a stable 4-bit sub-light glider under $O_h$ symmetry. However, a single species does not constitute a "zoo" or taxonomy. To understand if the 3D FCC lattice supports a diverse set of emergent particle-like states, we must systematically search for and classify other sub-light glider species.

### Key Objectives of this Experiment:
1. **Algorithmic Search:** Leverage the 48-element octahedral symmetry group ($O_h$) to perform a computationally efficient, symmetry-reduced exhaustive search of localized configurations of weight $4 \le W \le 8$ within a bounding box of $4 \times 4 \times 4$ on the 3D FCC lattice.
2. **Identification & Classification:** Track the preservation of bit-count, translational shape invariance, and sub-light velocity vectors to filter out chaotic, stationary, or speed-of-light states.
3. **Orbit Distinction:** Ensure that any newly found glider belongs to a distinct $O_h$ symmetry orbit, thereby confirming it as a truly unique "species" in our taxonomy rather than a rotated version of LUT-08.

By doing so, we will either expand the particle taxonomy of our 3D digital universe, or rigorously demonstrate that sub-light gliders on this lattice are highly constrained, rare, or unique.
