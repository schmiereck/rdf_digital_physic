The research is critically blocked. The evolutionary search for a rule that produces sustained motion is stalled at generation zero. Previous attempts to breed a population from an earlier lineage failed because that lineage had optimized for a flawed, short-term motion metric (iter_125, 127). A subsequent test on a fresh, random population (iter_127) was also a failure: 99% of the rules produced no motion, and the single rule that did was pathologically chaotic, yielding a near-zero fitness score. The evolutionary process cannot begin without a viable initial population containing at least some non-chaotic motion.

This reveals a fundamental problem in our rule generation strategy. The "density" of the rule (the number of non-identity mappings) appears to be a critical hyperparameter that we have not systematically explored. Previous experiments used either very "sparse" rules (random generation, typically 2-4 kernel pairs) which were too static, or "dense" rules (32+ mappings) which were too chaotic. The single chaotic-but-moving rule from iter_127 suggests we are near a phase transition.

The only logical path forward is to perform a systematic scan of this rule density hyperparameter. By generating and evaluating populations at different, fixed densities, we can determine if there is a "sweet spot" that balances stability and dynamics, producing the initial spark of sustained, non-chaotic motion required to seed the evolutionary algorithm.
