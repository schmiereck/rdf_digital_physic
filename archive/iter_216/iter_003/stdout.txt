The analysis document is written. Here's a summary of the key findings:

**Root cause:** `LateWindowDisplacementFitness` computes `fitness = displacement / (1 + bb_area)`. Since displacement is directly proportional to speed, the v=1c glider always scores the highest for any compact particle — it's mathematically the optimal solution for this objective. A v<c glider with speed 1/k earns only 1/k of the fitness of an equivalent v=c glider.

**Secondary issue:** The raw `center_of_mass` function wraps discontinuously on the toroidal grid, compressing the measured displacement when the particle crosses the boundary. This artefact partially obscured the true v=1c nature during iter_215, but didn't prevent convergence to it.

**The `SubLightFitness` design** fixes this with two mechanisms:
1. Hard rejection gates: `measured_velocity >= 0.9` → fitness=0; `period <= 1` → fitness=0
2. `period_bonus = 1 - 1/period` multiplier that makes internal oscillation fitness-relevant — a period-4 glider gets a 0.75× bonus vs. 0 for v=1c
