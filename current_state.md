Phase: Phase 2: Der "Zappel"-Detektor (Innere Schwingung)

## 1. Goal
Demonstrate that complex physical phenomena can emerge from minimal, local, reversible rules on a discrete, symmetric lattice.

## 2. Confirmed Findings
- **Rule Existence (1-bit):** 33 non-trivial, reversible, bit-conserving rules exist for a 1D, 3-bit neighborhood (iter_001).
- **v=c Gliders (1-bit):** 22 of the 33 1-bit rules produce stable gliders at the speed of light (iter_002).
- **Rule Existence (2-bit):** Non-trivial, reversible, bit-conserving rules exist for a 1D, 3-neighborhood, 2-bit/cell lattice (iter_003).
- **v=0 Oscillators (2-bit):** A minimal 2-bit/cell rule produces a stable, stationary, period-2 oscillation from a single-cell excitation (iter_004).
- **v < c Particles (2-bit):** A hybrid rule can be constructed to produce a stable glider with an effective velocity of v=c/2 by alternating oscillation and translation steps (iter_005).

## 3. Current Best Result
- **Fastest Particle:** v=c (1.0 cells/step) using a 1-bit rule (iter_002).
- **"Massive" Particle:** v=c/2 (0.5 cells/step) using a 2-bit rule with a 2-step cycle (iter_005).

## 4. In Progress
- **iter_006:** Attempting to construct a stable, propagating composite (two-cell) particle with v=c/2.

## 5. Open Questions
- Can stable, propagating, composite (multi-cell) particles exist?
- Is there a simple relationship between a composite particle's size/complexity and its range of possible velocities?
- What is the minimal rule set that allows for both stable single-cell and stable multi-cell gliders?
- What happens when a v=c/2 single-cell particle collides with a composite particle?
