# Current Research State

## 1. Goal
Demonstrate that complex physical phenomena can emerge from minimal, local, reversible rules on a discrete, symmetric lattice.

## 2. Status
**Phase 2: Der "Zappel"-Detektor (Dynamics)**
We have confirmed the existence of rules supporting pure translation (v=c) and pure stationary oscillation (v=0). We are now attempting to synthesize these two concepts.

## 3. Confirmed
- **Existence of 1-bit Rules:** There are 33 non-trivial, reversible, bit-conserving rules for a 1D, 3-bit neighborhood (iter_001).
- **v=c Gliders (1-bit):** 22 of the 33 1-bit rules produce stable, propagating gliders at the speed of light (v=c) from a single-bit initial condition (iter_002).
- **Existence of 2-bit Rules:** At least one non-trivial, reversible, bit-conserving rule exists for a 1D, 3-neighborhood, 2-bit/cell lattice (iter_003).
- **v=0 Oscillators (2-bit):** A minimal 2-bit/cell rule produces a stable, stationary, period-2 oscillation ("Zappeln") from a single-cell excitation (iter_004).

## 4. In Progress
- **iter_005:** Attempting to construct a hybrid rule that combines translation and oscillation to produce a stable particle with an effective velocity of v=c/2.

## 5. Open Questions
- Can we construct a rule that combines oscillation and translation to create a particle with v < c?
- What is the minimal rule that produces a stable v=c/2 glider?
- Is there a fundamental trade-off between a particle's internal state complexity and its propagation speed?
- How does the bit-conservation constraint affect the design of rules for v < c particles?
- What happens when two v < c particles collide?
